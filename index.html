How the numbers work

The county is divided into hexagonal cells of about 0.1 km². For each cell the model estimates the yearly loss from four hazards, then tests five adaptation measures and keeps the one with the highest net present value.

  • Expected yearly loss is property damage, days of lost business, the knock-on loss to the wider economy when a critical cell stops, and heat costs to households and employers.
  • Return is the present value of avoided losses divided by the capital cost plus upkeep, over the period and discount rate you set.
  • Tax base protected is shown separately and is not counted in the return.

This is a proof of concept. The geography is hand-drawn, the data is synthetic and the coefficients are placeholders. It shows what the tool does, not what Miami should build.
